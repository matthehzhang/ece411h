## 3. His conventions the tutor must teach in

### 3.1 The standard factors (10/21 table, [10/21 0:43:34–1:06:11])
"In general, $H(\omega)$ can be written in the form $H=A_1A_2\cdots A_n$" and "for any linear circuit, there will be a limited number of types of factors." [10/21 0:41:57–0:42:08]

| His name | Form | Magnitude straight line | Phase straight line | Circuit he gives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| constant $K$ | $K$ | flat at $20\log\lvert K\rvert$ | 0° if $K>0$; ±180° if $K<0$ | "the voltage scale" |
| zero @ origin | $(j\omega)^N$ | +20N dB/dec at all ω, through 0 dB at ω = 1 | flat +90°N, no ramp | $j\omega L$ |
| pole @ origin | $1/(j\omega)^N$ | −20N dB/dec, through 0 dB at ω = 1 | flat −90°N | $1/j\omega C$ |
| simple zero @ $\omega_z$ | $(1+j\omega/\omega_z)^N$ | 0 dB to $\omega_z$, then +20N dB/dec | 0° below $0.1\omega_z$, +45°N at $\omega_z$, +90°N above $10\omega_z$ | current into series R–L |
| simple pole @ $\omega_p$ | $1/(1+j\omega/\omega_p)^N$ | 0 dB to $\omega_p$, then −20N dB/dec | 0° → −45°N at $\omega_p$ → −90°N | RC divider, output across C |
| quadratic zero @ $\omega_z$ | $[1+j2\xi\frac{\omega}{\omega_z}+(\frac{j\omega}{\omega_z})^2]^N$ | 0 dB to $\omega_z$, then +40N dB/dec | 0° → +90°N at $\omega_z$ → +180°N, over $0.1\omega_z$–$10\omega_z$ | current into series R–L–C |
| quadratic pole @ $\omega_p$ | reciprocal of the above | 0 dB to $\omega_p$, then −40N dB/dec | 0° → −90°N → −180°N | $V_s$, series R and L, shunt C |

- **Leading 1 in every factor**, constants pulled out "including powers": $(200+j2\omega)^3\to200^3(1+j\omega/100)^3$; collect them into one boxed $K$ [video 9.19 26:05, 36:07]. Quadratic corner from the $\omega^2$ term ($16-\omega^2+j4\omega=16[1+j\omega/4+(j\omega/4)^2]$, $\omega_0=4$).
- **$\xi$ is lowercase xi**, "just the coefficient of the linear term… typically related to the resistance" [10/21 0:56:04]. He never draws $\xi$ into the asymptotes. Check it anyway: $\xi\ge1$ means two real corners (see §5, video 9.19(d)).
- **Order N is the exponent.** "$\omega_z$ is called a zero of $H(\omega)$, of order N (since $\omega=j\omega_z\Rightarrow N(\omega)=H(\omega)=0$)" [10/21 0:25:33]; a pole is where "$D(\omega)=0,\ H(\omega)\to\infty$" [10/21 0:40:09]. Names on the board: "simple zero @ $\omega_z$", "2nd order zero @ origin", "quadratic pole @ $\omega_p=1/\sqrt{LC}=\omega_0$", "3rd order simple pole @ $\omega_0=10^5$ rad/s" [11/02 0:24:22].
- **Read factors aloud on sight:** "we're gonna get a quadratic pole due to this factor in the denominator, and we're going to get a zero at the origin, right, plus some constant." [10/21 1:16:08]
- **Exam stems:** "an algebraic form that makes it very straightforward to identify the factors corresponding to any zeros or poles" (F23); "only factors corresponding to constants and simple or quadratic zeroes or poles" (F25 FE).

### 3.2 Axes, scales, units
- **$M(\omega)$[dB] $=20\log|H|$**, "log" = $\log_{10}$: "we're always gonna be working with log base tens here" [10/19 1:14:01]. "If we're talking about ratios of voltage or current, then it's a factor of 20 in front, not a factor of 10, and that's because the power goes as the voltage squared." [10/19 1:06:51]
- **"Bode plot" means the straight-line version:** "the way the Bode plot terminology is used in Ulaby, and therefore the way that we will use it here, a version of those plots where we make kind of linear approximations… will be what we strictly refer to as the Bode plot." [10/19 0:56:11; repeated 10/21 0:24:00] Magnitude dB vs log ω; phase linear vs log ω.
- **Ticks at $0.1\omega_c$, $\omega_c$, $10\omega_c$.** Origin factors: "there's no natural frequency scale for it. So we'll just say a frequency of one, 10, a 100." [10/21 0:46:57] "A decade is a factor of 10 change." [10/21 0:47:45]
- **"That vertical axis is not going through omega equal to zero."** [10/26 0:43:24] Start a decade or more below the lowest corner.
- **ω in rad/s.** Convert Hz explicitly: "These are just frequencies. They're not angular frequencies. So to get the angular frequency, we have to multiply… by two pi." [rec 11/02 0:28:59]
- **Phase in degrees** from 10/21 on (10/19 used radians, ±π/4). Peak-amplitude phasors throughout.

### 3.3 Magnitude straight lines
- Each factor: use the ω → 0 limit below its corner and the ω → ∞ limit above it; "we can sketch out very quickly… at the price of a little bit of loss in accuracy" [10/21 0:13:21–0:14:26]. The break is abrupt and exactly at the corner [video 9.19 27:15]. True curve at a simple corner is 3N dB off: "$20N\log|1+j|$… approximately 3N dB" [10/21 0:09:43].
- **Slopes add:** "the slope is just the sum of the slopes of the individual lines"; a constant only shifts the level [10/21 1:07:36]. In each interval set every factor below its corner to 1 and every factor above it to $j\omega/\omega_c$; slope = 20 × (net power of ω) dB/dec [video 9.19].
- **Every factor's slope persists above its corner.** He dropped this himself on 10/28 (§5).
- **Anchor on a known constant segment:** $M(0)$, else $M(\infty)$, else a mid band where the ω-dependence cancels [video 9.19 55:03]. The 10/21 table anchors origin factors at 0 dB at ω = 1; both give the same curve.

### 3.4 Phase straight lines
- "The criterion that we will use for much lower is that omega has to be less than point one times [$\omega_z$]", and above $10\omega_z$ use the high limit; the straight line between "has the advantage of going through the correct point at omega z." [10/21 0:22:05–0:23:21] A step at the corner is "a little too lousy of an approximation." [10/21 0:21:06–0:21:55]
- Slopes: ±45N°/dec for a simple factor, ±90°/dec per quadratic order, origin factors flat (no ramp) [video 9.19 51:25], constant flat. The total's slope changes at every $\omega_c/10$ and $10\omega_c$, so tick those on the phase axis [video 9.19 13:38, 30:54].
- **±180° branch:** "To determine which one of those possibilities we should use, we can look at what happens when omega is equal to omega naught." At $\omega_0$ the quadratic pole is $1/(j\omega_0RC)$, i.e. −90°, so it ends at −180° [10/28 0:18:07–0:19:05]. Continuity: $j^3$ is taken as 270°, "if you think about how this evolves as omega increases" [11/02 0:34:08–0:35:07].
- "the phases always appear in exponents… the phases just add up." [10/21 1:07:52–1:08:36]

### 3.6 Exact value at $\omega_0$ for a quadratic, drawn on top
- Video 9.33: substitute $\omega_c$ (the 1 and $(j\omega/\omega_c)^2=-1$ cancel), get $H(\omega_c)=10/j$, so $M=20$ dB, and draw a spike to 20 dB on top of the 0 dB / −40 dB/dec asymptotes [video 9.33 08:06–11:55]. For a quadratic pole $M(\omega_0)=20\log_{10}\frac{1}{2\xi}=20\log Q$.
- He states the limitation: "the shape of this is independent of the quality factor… one of the limitations of the linear approximation version of the Bode plots is that it doesn't capture these types of variations in the behavior near the resonant frequency." [10/28 0:24:20–0:25:42]
- **Band-pass keys draw the peak at the exact $|H(\omega_0)|$:** F23 SE2 P4 labels 0 dB while the asymptotes meet at −26.0 dB ($Q=20$); F24 FE P3 likewise. His 10/26 board put the vertex at 0 dB from $|H(\omega_0)|=1$, "a little shortcut" [10/26 0:51:28, 0:53:58]; the literal asymptote sum is $20\log(\omega_0RC)=-20\log Q$. **Rule:** draw the asymptotes, mark the exact value at $\omega_0$ with a dot and a number, and say which number is which.

### 3.7 Phase range and the quadrant rule
- **Exams state the range:** past stems give $-180°\le\phi\le180°$, $-180°\le\phi<180°$ and once $0°\le\phi<360°$; F23 SE2 stated none. Map the whole curve into the stated range. Shifting by 360° "is not gonna change anything"; otherwise keep the phase "closer to zero" [11/02 0:35:47–0:36:11].
- **His videos wrap only outside ±360°** ("if we go outside the range from minus three sixty to three sixty" [video 9.19 56:32]); 9.19(b) climbs to +360°. On the exam, wrap at ±180°. Do not copy the video rule.
- **Quadrant:** $\tan^{-1}$ must give "the actual correct phase… rather than being restricted to being between minus pi over two and pi over two" [10/19 0:09:52]. "Since the inverse tangent is typically defined as a function that… has a range of minus 90 degrees to plus 90 degrees, then if the actual correct phase is not within that range, then you're gonna have to add or subtract a 180 degrees to get the right phase." [10/26 1:11:17] **Rule: Re(denominator) < 0 → add 180° to the calculator angle.** The rationalized form, when its real part is positive, avoids the trap [10/26 0:03:52]. Phase of a fraction = phase(num) − phase(den) [10/19 0:49:04]. F23 FE 1(c) key: "(ii) 180°+tan⁻¹(b/a)".

### 3.8 Filter type, order, |H| limits, cutoff, resonance, Q
- **Type from impedance limits** (C open at DC, short at ∞; L the reverse), then voltage division: low-pass across C "at low frequencies… the impedance of the capacitor is high" [10/19 0:43:05]; high-pass across R "at high frequencies, the impedance of the capacitor goes to zero… so all the voltage drop will be across the resistor" [10/19 0:53:58]; high-pass across L "$Z_L\to0$ as $\omega\to0$, and $Z_L\to\infty$ as $\omega\to\infty$" [10/26 1:00:02]. Video habit: re-derive the limits from the circuit, "quite useful and important" [video 9.37 25:23–29:09].
- **Order (board, 10/28):** "the absolute value of the highest power of ω in the dominant behavior of $H(\omega)$ when ω is in the filter stop band." "You'll get an additional plus or minus 20 dB in that slope every time you increase the order by one." [10/28 0:33:15–0:34:26, 0:46:17] By his definition the series-RLC band-pass is first order in each stopband. "Much less than" ≈ one decade [10/28 0:44:33].
- **Passive |H|:** no gain element, so |H| > 1 needs resonance. Across R the peak is exactly 1 at $\omega_0$ [10/26 0:07:33]. Across L, $M(\omega_0)=\omega_0L/R=Q$ [10/26 1:11:51–1:13:51]; "if R goes to zero, the transfer function will go to infinity. But that will only happen if you're right around the resonant frequency." [10/19 0:27:33] Active stages: "a magnitude of the transfer function that is significantly in excess of one, or equivalently in excess of zero dB." [rec 11/02 0:24:25]
- **Cutoff = half power:** "frequency at the edge(s) of the passband at which $M(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}M_0$. Since power $\propto vi\sim v^2$, power at $\omega_c$ is half its peak value." [10/19 0:14:11] "Reduced by three dB compared to its maximum value." [10/19 1:04:13] $M_0$ = "maximum ('peak') non-resonant gain in filter's pass band" [10/19 0:12:11]; $B$ is defined for low-pass and band-pass only [10/19 0:19:09]. **Caveat:** for a quadratic or repeated pole the corner is not the −3 dB point; video 9.33 and the PS7 keys call $1/\sqrt{LC}$ the "cutoff" even where $M=+20$ dB. Say which definition you use.
- **Resonance:** "Frequency at which the input impedance is purely real; often corresponds to an extremum of $H(\omega)$." [10/19 0:25:02] $1/\sqrt{LC}$ only for series/simple topologies (PS7 9.2 gives $2.00\times10^4\ne1.41\times10^4$).
- **Q:** "Q quantifies the degree of frequency selectivity of a circuit and is related to the ratio of stored to dissipated energy", $Q=2\pi(W_{stor}/W_{diss})|_{\omega_0}$ [10/26 0:23:10]. Series RLC: $B=R/L$ [10/26 0:21:13]; $Q=\omega_0L/R=\omega_0/B=\frac1R\sqrt{L/C}$ [10/26 0:31:22, 1:12:17]; board: "high quality factor Q ⟺ narrow bandwidth B in general." (Lecture formulas. PS8 9.26 asks for these numbers: do not run them on 9.26 with him before 11/09.)

### 3.9 Active filters
- **Inverting stage:** lump the input branch into $Z_s$ and the feedback branch into $Z_f$; KCL at the inverting node with both currents written as leaving, $\frac{0-V_s}{Z_s}+\frac{0-V_{out}}{Z_f}=0\Rightarrow H=-Z_f/Z_s$, "which should look familiar to you because that's basically the result that we got for the inverting op amp amplifier circuit." [10/28 0:54:19–0:56:10]
- **Outputs act as voltage sources:** "the output of an op amp in the ideal op amp model can basically act as an ideal voltage source" [10/28 1:03:09]; "it really is acting as a voltage source. So it can… draw any amount of current… and they'll still give you the same voltage… the transfer functions get multiplied." [11/02 0:18:12–0:18:39] Passive: "connecting the output of one stage to the next stage would change the behavior of that first stage… So then you have to analyze the whole thing together." [11/02 0:18:39–0:19:47] A buffer is needed after a passive stage, not between inverting op-amp stages [10/28 1:04:22].
- **Order of work:** "start off by analyzing… the op amp that is closest to the input." [rec 11/02 0:03:47] Each inverting stage contributes −1 (180°); pairs cancel.
- **Board notes on the active low-pass [10/28 1:05:09–1:07:15]:** "$\omega_0$ doesn't depend on $R_s$"; "$V_{out}$ will be unchanged for any load resistance $R_L$"; "op-amp can provide gain in filter pass band." (That circuit is the Lab 5 topology: use the notes as P1 reasoning only; do not derive or check the Lab 5 $H$.)

### P3/P4, Bode: one procedure (10/19, 10/21, 10/26, 10/28 lectures + videos 9.19, 9.33, 9.37)
1. **Get $H=V_{out}/V_{in}$** by impedances: voltage division, or one KCL (output node, or op-amp inverting input with $v_-=v_+$). Multiply by 1 ($j\omega C/j\omega C$, $R/R$) to clear nested fractions. Divide by the input before writing $Me^{j\phi}$ (his 10/19 0:47:04 slip). Box $H$.
2. **Standard form:** constants out of every factor (with their powers), every factor starting with 1, quadratic corner from the $\omega^2$ term, one boxed $K$.
3. **Name each factor and its order**, origin factors included. For each quadratic compute $\xi$; $\xi\ge1$ → refactor into two real corners.
4. **Limits first:** $M(0)$, $M(\infty)$ in dB; $\phi(0)$, $\phi(\infty)$ in degrees, leaving ±180° open. Re-derive them from the circuit (C open/short, L short/open).
5. **Axes:** $M(\omega)$[dB] and $\phi(\omega)$ vs log ω, vertical axis at a small ω (not 0). Magnitude ticks at every corner; phase ticks at $\omega_c/10$, $\omega_c$, $10\omega_c$ of every factor; dashed verticals through both plots.
6. **Magnitude slopes, interval by interval:** factors below their corner = 1, above = $j\omega/\omega_c$; slope = 20 × net power of ω; breaks exactly at corners; every slope persists above its own corner.
7. **Anchor and level:** start from a known constant segment ($M(0)$, else $M(\infty)$, else a cancelling mid band); compute the dB value at every breakpoint.
8. **Second-order factors:** evaluate the exact $|H(\omega_0)|$ by substitution and mark it at $\omega_0$ (peak $20\log Q$, or the band-pass's exact value); label which value is exact and which is the asymptote.
9. **Phase ramps:** each simple factor 0 → ±90N° from $\omega_c/10$ to $10\omega_c$ (±45N° at $\omega_c$), each quadratic 0 → ±180° (±90° at $\omega_0$), origin factors flat ±90N°, constant 0/±180°. Sum the active slopes per interval; the total's slope changes at every /10 and ×10 mark.
10. **Fix the branch and range:** settle ±180° by continuity or by evaluating at $\omega_0$; apply the quadrant rule to any calculator angle (Re < 0 → +180°); map the whole curve into the stem's range (wrap at ±180°, not ±360°).
11. **Label everything:** plateau and breakpoint dB values, every non-zero slope "±20N dB/decade" with an arrow (phase slopes too, including 0°/dec, when asked), phase levels with a dashed final-value line. Check both ends against step 4.
12. **Read off what the stem asks:** type from limits and shape; cutoff (state −3 dB or corner); order by his stopband definition; maximum gain or design ratio; a "does it make sense" limit check.
